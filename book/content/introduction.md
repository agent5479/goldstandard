# Introduction

## What this book is

This book gathers the Gold Standard Dog Training website ({{siteUrl}}) into a single volume that can be read from start to finish, searched, printed, or kept beside you between sessions. Every public page of the site is reproduced as a chapter. The data behind the interactive tools is set out in full in the appendices: the glossary, the rehabilitation playbooks, the problem finder, the breed profiles, puppy development, and the exam question bank.

It is a companion to in-person coaching, not a replacement for it. The guide chapters explain *why* the method works the way it does and *what* to practise at home. The timing, feel, and calibration of the physical work are learned with your dog, in your environment, with Warwick beside you.

## Who it is from

Gold Standard Dog Training is run by Warwick Marshall from Takaka in Golden Bay, New Zealand. Warwick coaches dogs and their owners across the Tasman region. He works hands-on and in the places where behaviour actually happens: the driveway, the beach, the road verge, the front door, and the living room.

## The method in one paragraph

Gold Standard is a structured, relationship-based method that coaches the owner as much as the dog. It asks the handler to become a calm, consistent leader the dog can relax into, and it treats clear boundaries as a source of security rather than stress. Rewards come mainly through access, freedom, and a calm return to neutral, with food used selectively. Unwanted behaviour is interrupted in the act with a unique sound and touch, followed straight away by direction back to the right choice. Correction is physical but measured: leash, voice, and hand, calibrated to the individual dog and released the instant the message lands. Devices that deliver the correction for you are excluded: no prong collars, no shock, vibration, or remote collars, and no harnesses that teach pulling. Where a dog's social problems need feedback no human can give, sessions may use a balanced helper dog under strict safety conditions.

This places Gold Standard firmly within what is usually called *balanced* training, and at the more hands-on, handler-centred end of that family. It is not a force-free or positive-reinforcement-only method, and it does not claim to be. Warwick acknowledges the influence of Beckman-style training, but Gold Standard differs from it in important ways. The chapter [Where Gold Standard sits among training approaches](book:training-approaches) sets out those differences plainly, alongside what the research and New Zealand's welfare framework say, so that you can judge the fit for yourself before you book.

### Three commitments that anchor the method

Whatever else people debate about balanced training, three core standards sit at the foundation of every Gold Standard session and align directly with modern behavioural science:

- **No shock, prong, or vibration collars:** Mechanical pain devices are excluded from the method. Boundaries are communicated through handler posture, voice, and measured leash work — never outsourced to remote electronics.
- **Physiology first — medical rule-outs and autonomic regulation:** Behaviour is grounded in biology before it is ever treated as defiance. Veterinary checks for pain, thyroid function, or physical discomfort come first, paired with the handler's own nervous-system regulation so the dog is never reacting to an anxious human.
- **Functional substitution over drive suppression:** Rather than trying to crush genetic motor patterns, Gold Standard fulfils them with structured work — such as giving a retriever a deliberate carry ("mouth job") or channelling herding, scenting, and prey drives into purposeful outlets.

## How the book is organised

- **Part I: About Gold Standard** introduces the practice and Warwick's philosophy, and places the method among other training approaches.
- **Part II: Services** describes each coaching focus, from puppies and everyday manners through to reactive and complex cases.
- **Part III: The Client Guide** is the heart of the book: seven modules running from Foundation through Leadership, Understanding, Social needs, Training, and the Puppy phase to Daily life.
- **Part IV: Tools and Resources** introduces the website's interactive tools and the recommended equipment.
- **Part V: Service Areas** and **Part VI: Contact and Booking** cover where Warwick trains and how to get in touch.
- **The appendices** are reference compendiums: a glossary of the method's terms, the rehabilitation drivers and playbooks, the problem finder, a profile of every breed in the breed tools, puppy development stages with sample daily plans, and the full exam question bank with answers and explanations.

## How to read it

If you are considering coaching, read Part I and the Foundation module of the guide, then the [Important notice](book:disclaimer) at the front of the book. If you are already a client, work through the guide modules in order and return to the chapters your sessions focus on. The appendices are for looking things up rather than reading straight through.

Links in the text jump to the relevant chapter, section, or appendix entry. Sections marked **sessions only** describe techniques that must be demonstrated and calibrated in person; they are included so that you understand what you may see in a session and why, not so that you can try them unsupervised.
